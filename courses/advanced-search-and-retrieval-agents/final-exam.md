# Final Exam — Advanced Search & Retrieval Agents

> 20 multiple-choice. Closed-book. Pass = 16/20.
> Time: 60 minutes.

The final exam is one of six cert-criterion gates. The full set (course README §5):

1. **8/8 Build quizzes** at 4/5 or better.
2. **8/8 Build artefacts** produced and committed to the partner workspace.
3. **1 of 4 capstones** shipped (artefact deployed, demo rehearsed).
4. **This final exam** at 16/20 or above.
5. **Artefact review** passed against a rubric, with a Progress Solution lead.
6. **Live defence** — 90-minute walkthrough with a Solution lead on the chosen capstone.

The exam screens for synthesis across Builds; the artefact review is the *primary* gate because it tests delivery, not recall.

The exam draws across every Build. Answers from the per-Build quizzes are not sufficient; the exam tests the *synthesis* across Builds.

## Section A — Primitive Selection (Q1–Q5)

### 1. A customer wants a search experience on a 200,000-document policy library returning ranked results in under 250 ms p95. No synthesised answer; the user clicks through to read sources. Pick the primitive.

A. `/ask` streaming.
B. `/find`.
C. Retrieval Agent.
D. `/predict/chat`.

### 2. The same customer adds a second use case: their compliance officers ask multi-part questions like *"Does our X process comply with regulation Y given last quarter's amendment?"* Pick the primitive for this surface (the policy-browser stays as Q1's pick).

A. `/find` — same as the browser.
B. `/ask` sync — handles questions.
C. Retrieval Agent — multi-part questions need decomposition.
D. `/search` — returns metadata.

### 3. Build 1's trade-off matrix shows your tenant's agent endpoint at $0.18 median per call vs `/ask` sync at $0.008. For a public-facing chatbot answering single-fact product questions at 50,000 queries / month, the cost differential is:

A. Negligible.
B. About $8,500 / month — material; pick `/ask`.
C. About $90 / month — material but absorbable.
D. ARAG bills flat-rate; primitive choice doesn't matter.

### 4. The primitive-selection flowchart's first decision branch is:

A. *"Is the customer paying enough to use the agent?"*
B. *"Does the user expect a sentence-grade answer?"*
C. *"Is the corpus multimodal?"*
D. *"Does the tenant support `/predict/chat`?"*

### 5. The Retrieval Agent in conversational mode is the right primitive when:

A. Every conversational surface needs an agent.
B. At least one turn might need decomposition.
C. The customer asks for it.
D. The conversation is in a language other than English.

| 10 | B | Generated at query time from session state. |
| 11 | A | Chip strip for ≤ 4 filters, casual users. |
| 12 | B | Top-K; bottom-K spend is wasted. |
| 13 | B | Diminishing returns; built-in is the inflection. |

| 17 | B | Plan → execute → merge → synthesise. |
