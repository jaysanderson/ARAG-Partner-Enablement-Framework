# RFC — Performance Cycle Redesign FY30

**Author**: Maya Schwartz.
**Reviewers**: All business-unit VPs (Priya Anand, Marcus Ortiz, Aisha Okonkwo, Liam Hayashi, Dr Sara Vance), CEO, CFO.
**Status**: Open for comment.

## Problem

The current Atlas performance cycle (per `performance-review-framework.md`) has two structural issues:

1. **December year-end timing creates an outsized Q4 workload** for managers, particularly in Engineering and Customer Services where the same time window is incident-heavy and customer-renewal-heavy.
2. **Mid-year reviews are perfunctory** in practice. The intent is meaningful but managers treat them as a forms exercise. Calibration only matters at year-end.

In FY28 feedback, 67% of L4+ managers across business units reported the year-end timing was "a forced overlap with customer commitments" or stronger. Aisha Okonkwo and Marcus Ortiz independently flagged this as their #1 desired People-team change.

## Proposed redesign

Move to a four-touchpoint cycle on a calendar offset from customer/operational peak:

| Touchpoint | Timing | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 plan + objectives | February | Manager + employee define objectives for the next 12 months |
| Q2 lightweight check | May | 30-minute focused check, no ratings |
| Q3 deep mid-year | August | Full mid-year discussion + provisional rating |
| Q4 finalise + comp signal | November | Final rating, no compensation signal yet |
| Comp cycle | February (start of next year's Q1) | Coupled with Q1 plan |

Annual ratings shift from December to November. Compensation cycle remains February. The August deep mid-year becomes the substantive checkpoint, sized for actual reflection rather than form-filling.

### Calibration

- August: business-unit calibration before mid-year ratings shared with employees.
- November: business-unit + Maya Schwartz cross-org calibration for final ratings.
- Existing calibration discipline (Maya Schwartz observes Engineering, Customer Services and Compliance calibrations) preserved.

### Promotion timing

Decoupled from rating cycle. Promotion committees run quarterly (Engineering: Priya Anand and Marcus Ortiz alternating; Customer Services: Aisha Okonkwo; Compliance: Dr Sara Vance; Field Operations: Liam Hayashi; Sales: VP Sales; HR: Maya Schwartz). Promotion decisions reference latest rating but not constrained to year-end calendar.

## Risk

- Transition year: FY29-to-FY30 transition will have one 14-month cycle. Mitigated by communication and a small one-off comp adjustment for the transition.
- Customer perception: customer-facing comp signals shift one quarter. Sales-managed expectation only; no comp paid earlier or later than calendar year norms.
- Calibration load on Q3 (August) when EMEA traditionally has lower staffing density. Maya Schwartz's office to plan around this.

## Compatibility with related processes

- PIPs (`runbook-pip.md`) continue independently of the cycle.
- Internal mobility (`internal-mobility-policy.md`) decoupled from review timing.
- Acquisition integration (`runbook-acquisition-integration-people.md`) — acquired teams enter at the next available Q1 plan.

## Open questions

1. Should we move comp cycle earlier (to October) to avoid Q1 distraction? Argument for: cleaner. Argument against: signals risk before performance closes. Maya Schwartz tentatively prefers February still.
2. Lightweight Q2 check format — written, verbal, both? Aisha Okonkwo proposes verbal-only.
3. Does the cycle apply uniformly across regions or per-region offset? Proposal: global uniform with regional managers respecting local public-holiday density.

## Decision needed

CEO approval to pilot in one business unit FY29 (likely Engineering under Marcus Ortiz, which has volunteered), then full rollout FY30 conditional on pilot outcomes.
