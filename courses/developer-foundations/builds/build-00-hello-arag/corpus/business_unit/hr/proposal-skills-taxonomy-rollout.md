# Proposal — Atlas Skills Taxonomy Rollout

Prepared by Maya Schwartz's office in partnership with HR Operations. Decision needed: approve the FY29 rollout of the Atlas Skills Taxonomy across all 60,000 employees.

## Background

Atlas does not have a structured skills taxonomy. Roles are defined by titles and bands; the underlying skills are not catalogued. This creates friction in:

- Internal mobility (per `internal-mobility-policy.md`) — candidates and managers struggle to match.
- Learning budget alignment (per `learning-budget-policy.md`) — skill gaps not surfaced.
- Workforce-planning during mass hiring events (per `runbook-mass-hiring-event.md`).
- Acquisition integration skill-mapping (per `runbook-acquisition-integration-people.md`).
- Talent reviews — calibration is harder without a common vocabulary.

## Proposal

Implement the Atlas Skills Taxonomy in three phases over FY29.

### Phase 1: Engineering and Customer Services (Q1-Q2)

The two largest orgs. Skills cataloged for:
- All Engineering disciplines under Priya Anand and Marcus Ortiz (hardware design, distributed systems, cloud, embedded, etc.).
- All CSM and field-services skills under Aisha Okonkwo and Liam Hayashi (vertical knowledge, product mastery, customer-engagement skills).

### Phase 2: Compliance and Sales (Q3)

- Compliance skills under Dr Sara Vance (regulator knowledge, audit response, policy authorship).
- Sales skills (vertical mastery, methodology, enterprise vs commercial proficiency).

### Phase 3: HR and corporate functions (Q4)

- HR specialisations (TA, HRBP, Compensation, Learning).
- Corporate functions (Finance, Legal, IT, Sustainability).

## Inputs

- Existing skills inventories (Aisha Okonkwo's CSM org has the most mature inventory; serves as template).
- External taxonomies (Lightcast skills graph; SHL benchmark library; Burning Glass for industry specifics).
- Vendor-provided AI mapping (Workday Skills Cloud).

## Outputs

- Skills attached to every active employee profile in Workday.
- Skills attached to every job description.
- Skills attached to learning offerings.
- Internal mobility search supports skills as a search dimension.

## Cost

- Workday Skills Cloud module: $240,000 setup + $180,000/year.
- External taxonomy licenses: $90,000/year.
- Implementation labour (consulting): $420,000 one-time.
- Internal labour (HR Ops, business-unit People Partners): equivalent of 2 FTE for the year.

## Outcomes expected

- 30% reduction in internal-mobility time-to-fill.
- 20% improvement in learning-spend alignment with skill gaps.
- Compliance audit readiness for skills-based hiring evidence (anticipating regulator trends).

## Risk

- "Skill inflation" — employees overstating skills. Mitigated by manager review and peer attestation patterns Aisha Okonkwo piloted in FY28.
- Vendor lock-in to Workday Skills Cloud. Mitigated by export-friendly architecture commitment in vendor MSA.
- Privacy concerns under `data-handling-hr-supplement.md` and EMEA `GDPR`. Reviewed by Dr Sara Vance; managed as Internal-class data.

## Decision sought

Approval of $930,000 year-1 budget and 2.0 internal FTE equivalent for FY29 rollout. Phase gating retains exit option if Phase 1 outcomes underdeliver.
