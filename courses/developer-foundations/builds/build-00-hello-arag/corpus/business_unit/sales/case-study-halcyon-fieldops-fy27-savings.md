# Case Study — Halcyon FieldOps FY27 Operational Savings

**Customer:** Halcyon Logistics
**Industry:** 3PL (NORAM)
**Primary CSM:** Marcus Ortiz
**Atlas products:** Atlas FieldOps, Atlas Logix

## The headline

In Halcyon's FY27, Atlas FieldOps (2,400 dispatcher-issued devices across 14 NORAM hubs) combined with Atlas Logix routing produced approximately USD 4.1M in operational savings against Halcyon's pre-Atlas FY24 baseline. The savings figure was independently verified by Halcyon's CFO office and is the headline number in the FY28 FieldOps renewal proposal.

## How the savings break down

| Category | FY27 savings (USD) |
|---|---|
| Fuel — routing optimisation | 1,840,000 |
| Driver overtime reduction | 1,100,000 |
| Re-dispatch / failed-delivery rework | 690,000 |
| Inventory-visibility improvements (warehouse cross-checks) | 470,000 |

Total: USD 4.1M.

## What made it work

- Atlas Logix routing engine running at 99.92% reliability post-INC-2027-0188 closure.
- Atlas FieldOps device fleet at 99.7% sync reliability over the trailing 12 months (closed post-INC-2028-0034).
- Marcus Ortiz's architecture review at each hub-expansion phase kept the integration footprint clean.

## What did not contribute

- No headcount reduction at Halcyon. The CFO office was explicit that Atlas's contribution was efficiency at constant headcount, not labour displacement. This framing matters for the Halcyon AE; future expansion conversations should follow the same line.

## The follow-on commercial wins

The FY27 savings figure anchors three FY28 deals:

- proposal-halcyon-fieldops-renewal.md — 3-year renewal at 19% discount, USD 7.3M Year 1.
- proposal-halcyon-logix-volume-uplift.md — custom volume pricing, USD 880k Year 1.
- proposal-halcyon-bldhub-warehouse-pilot.md — cross-product pilot.

## What this case shows

- Independent customer verification of savings numbers gives an AE a defensible headline that survives executive-level scrutiny.
- The "efficiency at constant headcount" framing is sales-friendly because it removes the political objection most logistics CFOs have to vendor-led "savings" pitches.
