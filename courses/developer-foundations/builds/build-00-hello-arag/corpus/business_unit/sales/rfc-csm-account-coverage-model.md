# RFC · CSM Account Coverage Model FY29

**Status:** Draft for comment.
**Authors:** VP Customer Success, VP Sales.
**Reviewers:** Aisha Okonkwo, Liam Hayashi, Priya Anand, Marcus Ortiz, Dr Sara Vance.

## Problem statement

Three of Atlas's five strategic-customer CSM ownerships are dual-hatted with engineering or compliance executive roles: Priya Anand (Norvale, also VP Engineering), Aisha Okonkwo (Cresta, also Director of Customer Success), Dr Sara Vance (Meridian, also Chief Compliance Officer). The dual-hat model has worked well operationally (case-study-cross-product-expansion-playbook.md identifies CSM continuity as a force multiplier), but creates a single-point-of-failure risk and constrains the executives' capacity for other strategic work.

## Proposed change

Introduce a "shadow CSM" role per strategic account. The shadow CSM holds the operational relationship cadence (QBRs, mid-cycle health checks, escalation triage), while the dual-hat executive retains the strategic relationship.

Specifically:

- Norvale Energy — Priya Anand strategic, shadow CSM TBD.
- Cresta Health Network — Aisha Okonkwo strategic + operational currently; shadow CSM frees her for broader CS leadership.
- Meridian Bank — Dr Sara Vance strategic, shadow CSM TBD.
- Halcyon Logistics — Marcus Ortiz strategic, shadow CSM TBD.
- Talos Steelworks — Liam Hayashi strategic + operational; status quo proposed (Liam's APAC field-operations dual hat is more bandwidth-positive than negative).

## Why now

- The five strategic customers' cumulative pipeline (across 15 active proposals) has tripled over 18 months. Executive CSM bandwidth is constraining velocity.
- The Pulse-Grid promotion + Q4 close cycle will further compress executive availability.

## What success looks like

- Executive CSMs spend more time in commercial-strategy conversations and less time in operational triage.
- Shadow CSMs reach the level of customer trust where the customer accepts them as a viable escalation path before the executive intervenes.
- No deterioration in strategic-customer NPS.

## Open questions

1. Should shadow CSMs report into the strategic-CSM dual-hat executive, or into a separate CS hierarchy?
2. How is account commission split between strategic and shadow CSMs?
3. Should the shadow CSM role be a development pathway for high-potential mid-level CSMs?

## Risks

- Customer perception of CSM "downgrade" if the introduction is mishandled.
- Erosion of the strategic-customer trust that comes from a single executive relationship.

## Decision deadline

Comments by 31 July 2028. Decision at the FY29 sales-kickoff in Q4.
