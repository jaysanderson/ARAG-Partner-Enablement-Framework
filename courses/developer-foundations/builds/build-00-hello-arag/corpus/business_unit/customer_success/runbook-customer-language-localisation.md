# Customer Success Runbook · Customer Language Localisation

**Owner:** Liam Hayashi (Head of Field Operations) — co-authored with Aisha Okonkwo
**Region:** Global (especially APAC and EMEA)
**Origin:** Codified following `escalation-talos-fieldops-language-pack.md`

## Principle

Customer-language localisation is a customer-success retention lever, not a product feature. Treat language gaps as relationship risks and act accordingly.

## When to escalate a language-localisation request

- Customer-side written request via the CSM.
- CSM identifies a language gap during on-site visit, customer training, or incident response.
- Customer NPS data shows language-correlated drop.

## Process

1. CSM logs the request and tags it as a customer-success-driven product feature.
2. CSM brings the request to the product roadmap review with engineering leadership (typically Priya Anand for product-side approvals).
3. CSM proposes a delivery date.
4. If the customer is the lead translation-review partner, the CSM coordinates the translation cadence.
5. Atlas's commercial team is informed that the customer is not to be charged for the localisation as a feature upgrade — they are the partner that pushed the feature.

## Pricing posture

- Customer that triggers the localisation: no charge.
- Other customers who benefit: standard product-feature pricing where applicable.

## Translation review cadence (typical)

- Week 1-2: Initial translation pass by Atlas's translation contractor.
- Week 3-4: Customer-side translation review.
- Week 5-6: Translation refinement.
- Week 7-8: Atlas-side UX integration testing.
- Week 9-10: Beta deployment at customer site.
- Week 11-12: General availability rollout.

## Languages currently supported (Q3 2028)

- Atlas FieldOps: English, German, French, Dutch, Italian, Spanish, Japanese.
- Atlas BuildingHub: English, German, French, Dutch, Spanish.
- Atlas Pulse: English, German, Dutch.
- Atlas Grid: English, German, Dutch, Spanish.
- Atlas Logix: English, German, Dutch.
- Atlas E-220 maintenance documentation: English, German, French, Dutch, Japanese.
- Atlas Aura: English, German, Dutch.
- Atlas Ledger: English (no localisation requirement to date).

## Roadmap (Q4 2028)

- Atlas BuildingHub Japanese (driven by potential Cresta-equivalent APAC customer).
- Atlas Pulse French (driven by EDF early conversations).

## Anti-patterns

- Treating localisation as a sales feature rather than a customer-success investment.
- Charging the lead customer for the feature they pushed.
- Late delivery. Localisation commitments must be honoured on the date promised; slipping signals lack of seriousness.

## Quote attributed to Liam Hayashi

> "If a customer's technicians cannot read our error messages, our product is unsafe at their sites."
