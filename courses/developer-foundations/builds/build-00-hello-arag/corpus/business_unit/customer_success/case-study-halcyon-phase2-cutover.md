# Case Study · Halcyon Logistics Phase 2 APAC Cutover

**Customer:** Halcyon Logistics
**Region:** NORAM HQ / APAC operationally
**Primary CSM:** Marcus Ortiz
**Programme:** Atlas Logix Phase 2 cutover (Singapore + Sydney origin routes)

## Background

Following INC-2027-0188 (the Logix routing engine memory leak), Halcyon's expansion to Phase 2 (APAC routes) was treated as a high-stakes engineering exercise. Andre Pellegrini, Halcyon COO, was personally engaged in the risk review. Inge Larsson — the EMEA Operations Director — was newly appointed and watching closely.

## What made it work

1. **Marcus on-site.** Marcus Ortiz embedded in Halcyon Singapore office during cutover week. The customer's recall of having an Atlas principal engineer-architect at their elbow during the cutover shaped their expansion decision.
2. **Priya Anand as guarantor.** Bringing the VP Engineering to the daily executive standup demonstrated Atlas's commitment at a level the customer's COO understood.
3. **Doubled-up parallel run.** 30 days instead of the standard 7 — accepted on Marcus's recommendation, without contractual fight from Atlas commercial.
4. **Version-pinned, no compromise.** Only Atlas Logix 4.8.0. No 4.7.x deployments anywhere in the Halcyon estate.

## Sequence

- 2028-01-22: Phase 2 contract signed.
- 2028-04-20: Andre Pellegrini's 22-bullet risk register surfaces.
- 2028-04-23: Atlas restructures cutover plan around customer concerns.
- 2028-05-04: Cutover executed without incident, Marcus on-site.
- 2028-06-04: Production-of-record declared.
- 2028-06-15: Phase 3 EMEA approved.

## Quote attributed to Andre Pellegrini

> "Marcus + Priya turning up was worth more than any contractual remedy."

## Implications

- Engineering presence during high-stakes cutovers is a non-negotiable for Tier-1 customers.
- The 30-day parallel-run pattern is now standard for Tier-1 Atlas Logix cutovers.
- Customer-led risk registers should be treated as gifts, not threats.
