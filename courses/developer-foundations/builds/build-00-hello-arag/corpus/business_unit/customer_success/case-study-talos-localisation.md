# Case Study · Talos Steelworks Localisation Win

**Customer:** Talos Steelworks
**Region:** APAC (Japan)
**Primary CSM:** Liam Hayashi
**Programme:** Atlas FieldOps Japanese localisation

## The narrative

After INC-2028-0034, the Talos plant maintenance team's FieldOps adoption dropped sharply. The root cause was not the incident itself — that had been cleanly remediated via RB-FieldOps-Sync-004 — but the fact that Talos technicians had found the English-only error messages during remediation impossible to action without Liam Hayashi's translation help on the phone.

Yui Nakajima formally requested complete Japanese localisation of the FieldOps technician application within 90 days, citing the incident response experience as proof that English-only UX was now a safety issue at her sites.

## What Liam Hayashi did

1. Validated the request was reasonable and product-line-strategic.
2. Brought it to Priya Anand's product roadmap review — got Japanese-language pack moved from Q4 2028 to Q2 2028.
3. Personally project-managed Talos as the lead translation-review partner.
4. Negotiated USD 0 cost for the language pack — Talos would not be charged for being the partner that pushed the feature.

## Outcomes

- Talos NPS jumped from 73 to 84 (Q2 2028 measurement).
- Talos confirmed Atlas Pulse Q3 2028 pilot (8 units) and Atlas Grid evaluation onward.
- The Japanese language pack shipped 2028-04-29 — also benefiting 7 other Atlas customers across APAC.
- Yui Nakajima quoted in Talos's internal review: "Liam-san treated our safety concern as Atlas's safety concern."

## Why this case study matters

Localisation is often treated as a product feature. The Talos story reframes it as a customer-success retention lever — and a multiplier for other customers in the region. The Japanese pack would not have shipped in Q2 2028 without Liam's escalation. And every APAC customer benefited.

## Lessons captured

- Localisation gaps are safety-and-trust issues, not UI polish issues.
- Customer success has a legitimate seat at the product roadmap table.
- "We don't charge you for being the partner who pushed the feature" is a powerful relationship statement.
