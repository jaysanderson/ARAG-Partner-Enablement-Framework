# Case Study · Norvale Energy Pulse Expansion Story

**Customer:** Norvale Energy
**Region:** EMEA
**Product focus:** Atlas Pulse
**Primary CSM:** Priya Anand
**Programme:** Pulse pilot to 12-unit expansion

## The expansion arc

Norvale was the lead-customer reference for the Atlas Pulse product. The expansion arc from pilot (4 units, 2028) to full deployment (12 units, 2029) is one of the most-studied customer-success programmes in Atlas's portfolio.

## What made it work

1. **Engineering-led trust.** Priya Anand owned the customer relationship as VP Engineering — not a sales hand-off after contract signing.
2. **Visible accountability during stress.** When the telemetry-gap escalation hit (April 2028), Priya turned up in person at Norvale Rotterdam on Day 2. The customer's recall of that moment shaped their expansion decision more than any feature pitch could have.
3. **Product evolution driven by customer feedback.** The MQTT broker high-availability reference architecture was first deployed at Norvale, in direct response to their pilot escalation.
4. **Respect for customer caution.** When Norvale's procurement committee paused Grid Phase 1 pending Pulse stability re-confirmation, Priya accepted the pause without commercial pushback.

## Sequence of events

- 2027-11-30: Pilot contract signed.
- 2028-04-01: First 2 Pulse units commissioned.
- 2028-04-17: Telemetry-gap escalation (see `escalation-norvale-pulse-pilot-stall.md`).
- 2028-05-22: HA broker architecture deployed.
- 2028-06-04: 12-unit expansion (2029) greenlit by Norvale investment committee.
- 2028-06-04: Grid pilot also greenlit.

## Quote attributed to Norvale's investment-committee chair

> "Priya treated our caution as wise, not insulting. That's why we said yes."

## Implications for the Atlas customer success function

- The engineering-led CSM model works at scale for the right customer profile.
- Pilot-stress responses can be net-positive for expansion if handled with visible accountability.
- Product evolution feeding back from a single customer's pain can become the standard architecture for the whole product line.
