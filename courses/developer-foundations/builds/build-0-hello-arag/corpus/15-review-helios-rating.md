# Gear Review — Helios Down Jacket Temperature Rating Analysis (by Mara Chen)

This piece is a more technical review than my standard ambassador content. It addresses one specific question: **is the Helios down jacket actually rated as advertised?** Across four shoulder-season seasons in Patagonia, Tasmania, and the Cascades, I've taken field temperature data on the Helios in a range of layering combinations.

## Methodology

I carried a wrist-worn thermometer and logged ambient temperature + apparent comfort across 41 mornings on Patagonia W trips, Tasmania Overland traverses, and Cascade weekenders. I noted: outer layer, base layer, comfort level (1-5), and any insulation-relevant factors (wind, humidity, exertion within the previous hour).

## What the marketing says

The Helios is marketed as warm down to:

- **Active warmth (over a midweight base, hiking):** -5 °C.
- **Static warmth (inside a hardshell, standing):** -15 °C.

## What I measured

**Active warmth scenario, midweight base layer + Helios + hiking:**

The advertised -5 °C is honest. On 23 morning measurements between -3 and -6 °C with mild wind (under 15 km/h), I rated comfort at 4/5 or 5/5 in 21 of them. The two outliers were both in 25%+ humidity with wind — exterior dampness reduced the Helios's loft. In dry cold, the rating holds.

**Static warmth scenario, midweight base + Helios + hardshell + standing:**

The advertised -15 °C is honest down to wind chill effects. On 11 morning measurements between -12 and -18 °C ambient with light wind, I rated 4/5 comfort in 9 of them. The two below-comfort were both with apparent temperature (wind chill included) dropping below -22 °C. At -15 °C ambient without significant wind, the Helios + hardshell setup is functional.

## What surprises me

The Helios's wind performance is better than its weight suggests. 380 g of down has no business holding warmth in 30 km/h gusts; the Helios does, because the 10 D shell breaks wind effectively despite its low denier.

## Comparison

Vs. the Patagonia Macro Puff (similar weight, synthetic): the Helios wins in dry cold, loses in wet cold. If you live in Pacific Northwest fog, consider synthetic. If you live in continental cold, the Helios is the right answer.

## Don't do this

**Don't wear the Helios under a heavy pack on multi-day trips.** Compression destroys down loft. Stuff the Helios into your pack during hiking, wear it at camp. See full layering guidance in `03-product-helios-jacket.md`.
