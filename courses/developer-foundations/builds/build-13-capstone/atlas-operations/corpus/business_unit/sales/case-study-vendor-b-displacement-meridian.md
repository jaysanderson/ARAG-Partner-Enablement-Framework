# Case Study · Vendor B Displacement at Meridian

**Customer:** Meridian Bank
**Industry:** Financial Services (NORAM)
**Primary CSM:** Dr Sara Vance
**Atlas products:** Atlas Ledger

## The headline

In 2025, Meridian Bank replaced its incumbent Vendor B financial-platform with Atlas Ledger in the first deployed market. The displacement was won on a single commercial differentiator: Atlas's no-surcharge SOX-compliant deployment versus Vendor B's ~12% SOX surcharge. The reference is the most durable competitive asset Atlas Sales owns in NORAM banking.

## The competitive dynamic at the time

- Vendor B was the incumbent; the platform had been live at Meridian for ~6 years.
- Switching cost was high; Meridian's procurement team had assigned an initial 0.6 probability to a successful displacement.
- Vendor B's SOX surcharge was visible in their pricing tables and not negotiable below 10% across multi-market deployments.

## What Atlas led with

1. The SOX no-surcharge positioning, in writing in the proposal.
2. The Atlas Ledger control-plane audit-resilience demonstration (Dr Sara Vance led the technical walkthrough).
3. A migration sequencing plan that respected Meridian's FY26 audit calendar.

## What Atlas did not do

- Atlas did not undercut Vendor B on raw license cost. The Atlas Ledger NORAM list price was within 5% of Vendor B's discounted offer. The win was on TCO and compliance, not on per-market license price.

## The follow-through

The 2025 displacement is the source of every subsequent Meridian deal:

- Atlas Ledger 5-market expansion (2026-2027).
- Atlas Grid Phase 1 + Phase 2 (USD 1.9M Year 1).
- Atlas Ledger Multi-Market Add-On (proposed Q2 2028).
- Atlas Aura data-centre cooling pilot (proposed Q2 2028).
- Atlas FieldOps back-office tech pilot (proposed Q2 2028).

## What this case shows

- A single sharp commercial differentiator (no SOX surcharge) can displace an incumbent in a high-switching-cost segment if the technical credibility is real.
- The displacement reference is worth more, in subsequent deal cycles, than the original deal itself.
- AEs prospecting NORAM banking should lead with the Meridian reference and POL-PRICING-v4.0's explicit no-surcharge clause.

## Vendor B's current posture

Vendor B has since dropped its public SOX surcharge in NORAM banking deals (Atlas tracking suggests this happened in Q1 2027). The Meridian displacement reference still holds because Vendor B's TCO over a multi-year deployment remains higher.
