# Case Study · Talos Pulse Pilot Conversion

**Customer:** Talos Steelworks
**Industry:** Heavy Industry (APAC)
**Primary CSM:** Liam Hayashi
**Atlas products:** Atlas E-220, Atlas FieldOps, Atlas Pulse (converting)

## The headline

Talos Steelworks moved from a 4-unit Pulse pilot to an 8-unit Year-1 expansion in three quarters, with a Year-2 Grid SOW already drafted. The pilot's conversion came in a tougher APAC competitive environment than the equivalent EMEA conversions and is the reference case for selling Pulse in heavy industry.

## The starting point

Talos was an Atlas E-220 customer with 12 turbines across two production sites. The relationship was stable but had not expanded beyond turbines + a modest FieldOps deployment. Pulse-class storage was a net-new conversation initiated by Liam Hayashi during the Q2 2027 QBR.

Vendor B had a competing offering with stronger APAC commercial presence. Atlas's pitch leaned on the integrated generation-storage story already proved at Norvale and the existing operational trust from the E-220 fleet (99.91% trailing-12-month uptime).

## The pilot

- 4 Pulse units, two sites.
- Pilot pricing: 30% per the standard pilot framework.
- Engineering lead: Priya Anand (Pulse product owner).
- Operational lead: Liam Hayashi (Talos relationship + APAC field operations).

## The cooling-regression overlap

INC-2027-0142 (turbine cooling regression) affected one Talos site during the early pilot months. Atlas closed the incident via RB-E220-Cooling-001 without it bleeding into the Pulse pilot evaluation. The clean separation between the two product responses was credibility-positive.

## The conversion

proposal-talos-steelworks.md (Year-1 expansion) closed at 17% discount. EUR 1.49M Year 1 contract value. The Year-2 Grid SOW (proposal-talos-grid-y2-sow.md) is now drafted.

## The FieldOps inflection

The Talos FieldOps deployment was affected by INC-2028-0034 (offline-sync corruption) during the conversion negotiation window. Liam Hayashi managed the dual-track conversation — Pulse conversion on one track, FieldOps incident response on the other — without losing momentum on either.

The Talos FieldOps Renewal subsequently landed with the managed-service tier added explicitly to address Talos's post-incident preferences.

## What this case shows

- Even in tougher competitive geographies, the integrated-product story (generation + storage + distribution) is defensible if backed by operational trust.
- A concurrent incident on a different product doesn't have to derail a conversion if the CSM holds the narrative.
- Pilot-to-conversion velocity matters; the 9-month conversion is faster than the typical 12-month assumption and earned more favourable pricing terms.
