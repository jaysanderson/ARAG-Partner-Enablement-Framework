# Case Study · Meridian Bank Atlas Ledger Phased Rollout

**Customer:** Meridian Bank
**Industry:** Financial Services (NORAM)
**Primary CSM:** Dr Sara Vance
**Atlas products:** Atlas Ledger, Atlas Grid, Atlas Pulse (proposed)

## The headline

Atlas Ledger landed at Meridian Bank in 2025 as a single-market SOX-compliance proof point. Three years on, the product is live in 5 markets, the bank has greenlit a 4-market multi-market add-on, and the relationship has stretched into Atlas Grid (Phase 2 live) plus a proposed Atlas Pulse expansion. The Meridian portfolio is now Atlas's reference for cross-product banking deployment.

## Why Meridian chose Atlas in 2025

Vendor B was the incumbent for the finance-platform layer at Meridian. The Atlas advantage was the SOX-compliant deployment story: no surcharge for SOX (a Vendor B surcharge of ~12% existed at the time), and the Atlas Ledger control plane was demonstrably auditable.

Dr Sara Vance, then a director-level compliance officer, led the technical evaluation and has been the primary CSM since the original deal.

## The expansion arc

- **2025:** Single-market deployment. Marcus Ortiz consulted on architecture.
- **2026:** Two additional markets, taking the deployment to three. Standard pricing.
- **2027:** Two further markets. Phase 1 Atlas Grid pilot launched on the energy side.
- **Q1 2028:** Atlas Grid Phase 2 contract signed (USD 1.9M Year 1).
- **Q2 2028:** Multi-Market Add-On (4 new markets) + Aura data-centre cooling pilot proposed.

## The Logix moment

Meridian does not use Atlas Logix. However, the routing-engine memory-leak incident (INC-2027-0188) elsewhere had a downstream effect on the Meridian relationship: Meridian's CIO referenced Atlas's handling of that incident (rapid Marcus Ortiz ownership; RB-Logix-MemLeak-002 published) as a contributing factor in the Phase 2 Atlas Grid approval. Cross-product reputational effects matter.

## Compliance posture as commercial lever

The SOX §404 deployment characteristics are the commercial differentiator. The GDPR controller-processor delineation, refreshed at each market addition, has become a credibility builder with Meridian's UK and EU subsidiary executives.

## What this case shows

- Compliance posture is a sales lever, not just a defensive obligation.
- Cross-product reputation events (e.g., Logix incident handling) can land at adjacent accounts.
- The same CSM (Dr Sara Vance) carrying a relationship across multiple Atlas product launches is a force multiplier — discontinuity in account ownership would cost expansion velocity.
