# Case Study — Talos Steelworks Multi-Site Mass Hire (APAC)

Liam Hayashi's APAC Field Operations org hired 60 technicians in 90 days in FY28 to support the Talos Steelworks multi-site rollout. This case study documents the people-side execution against the Mass Hiring Event Runbook.

## Context

Talos Steelworks committed to a phased Atlas FieldOps deployment across 14 sites in Japan, South Korea, Vietnam and Thailand. The deployment timeline required FieldOps technicians on the ground in each region within 6 months of contract signing. APAC Field Operations needed to scale from 80 to 140 technicians.

## Execution against runbook

### T-30: business case and approval

CFO approved the headcount expansion against the Talos contract margin per the Atlas Pricing & Discount Policy `POL-PRICING-v4.0`. Maya Schwartz approved the People plan. Headcount opened across Singapore (HQ), Tokyo, Seoul, Ho Chi Minh City and Bangkok.

### T-21: TA squad

7 recruiters formed the squad, including 2 specialist heavy-industry recruiters seconded from a search partner. Liam Hayashi attended weekly review personally; regional field leads (Bangkok, HCMC, Seoul, Tokyo, Singapore) were the hiring managers.

### T+0: pipeline open

- Atlas careers page localised in 4 languages.
- Heavy referral surge — referral bonus doubled per runbook. Atlas APAC employee base ran a "Talos Talent Tuesday" referral drive that yielded 22% of final hires.
- Local technical institutes engaged (Singapore Polytechnic, Tokyo Tech, KAIST, HCMC University of Technology, KMUTT Bangkok).
- Industry channels via the Asian Iron and Steel Institute network.

### Quality control

- Maya Schwartz's sampled offer audit caught one band misalignment in week 4 (Seoul Korean Won conversion error). Corrected immediately.
- Diversity-of-slate monitoring showed under-representation of women technicians; targeted outreach to Women in Manufacturing APAC network for the back half of the program.
- Adverse-impact analysis on rejection patterns: clean.

### Onboarding

- `onboarding-field-operations-liam-org.md` was pre-staged in 4 languages.
- Buddy bench scaled — Liam's existing senior techs each took 2 buddies (compensated with extra PTO for the buddy load).
- Safety training (`safety-policy-field-operations.md`) was the longest-pole item; FR PPE and heat-stress training had to be scheduled across 5 sites.
- Atlas FieldOps app training scaled by doubling the live cohort capacity and adding asynchronous content.

### Wrap

- 58 hires made in 90 days against 60 target. 2-hire shortfall was absorbed by re-pacing the Bangkok site's deployment schedule.
- Time-to-fill average 38 days (target was 45).
- 90-day retention 96% (target was 92%).
- Offer-accept rate 78% (above 70% target).

## Cross-function coordination

- Compliance (Dr Sara Vance): heavy-industry safety regulator mapping for each country. No compliance issues raised.
- Customer Success (Aisha Okonkwo): joint Talos account pod structure agreed before the hires arrived, so each new technician knew which CSM they'd partner with.
- Engineering (Marcus Ortiz): Atlas FieldOps app refresher in week 1 for the new technicians, delivered by his Logix-adjacent engineering bench.

## Lessons

1. The runbook held up — minimal improvisation needed.
2. Referral surge worked far better than expected; doubling the bonus paid back ~10x.
3. Safety training was the actual bottleneck, not technical hiring.
4. Maya Schwartz's sampled offer audit caught a costly error in week 4.
5. Liam's cross-region buddy bench was the critical resource; without his existing senior tech depth, the program would have failed quality control.

## Follow-on

The FY29 plan to extend Atlas FieldOps deployment to a second tier of Talos sites is now de-risked from a people-side perspective; the bench exists, the playbook is proven, and the runbook is calibrated.
