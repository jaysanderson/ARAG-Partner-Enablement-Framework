# HR Runbook — Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)

This runbook governs the operational execution of a PIP at Atlas. Owned by Maya Schwartz with HRBPs as day-to-day operators.

## Principles

1. A PIP is a genuine remediation tool, not a stealth-firing mechanism. Maya Schwartz audits PIP success rates quarterly to enforce this.
2. PIPs are written, time-bound, and have explicit measurable exit criteria.
3. The employee receives the support they need to succeed.

## When this runbook fires

- Sustained underperformance evidenced by the most recent performance review (rating Below).
- OR a documented pattern of failure to meet role expectations over a minimum 90-day observation window.
- Never as a first-time response to a single performance gap — coaching and growth plans precede.

## Steps

### Step 1: Decision to PIP (T-7 days)

- Manager + HRBP align on PIP necessity.
- Skip-level approval mandatory. For L4+ employees, business-unit VP approval (Priya Anand / Marcus Ortiz / Aisha Okonkwo / Liam Hayashi / Dr Sara Vance / Maya Schwartz).
- Standard PIP duration: 60 days for L1-L3, 90 days for L4+.

### Step 2: PIP plan drafted

- Specific measurable objectives tied to role expectations.
- Weekly check-in cadence defined.
- Support and resources specified (mentor, training, workload adjustment).
- Exit criteria clearly stated.
- Reviewed by HRBP and HR Legal before delivery.

### Step 3: PIP delivered (T+0)

- Manager + HRBP present. Employee receives written PIP and time to read.
- Employee given 2 business days to acknowledge and raise concerns or questions.
- No coercion to "accept" — acknowledgment is administrative.

### Step 4: Weekly check-ins

- Manager runs weekly 1:1 specifically against PIP objectives.
- HRBP attends week 1, mid-point and final check-in.
- Documentation maintained — Workday PIP module.

### Step 5: Mid-point review

- Formal mid-point check between manager, employee and HRBP.
- Clear signal on trajectory: on track / mixed / off track.
- Recalibration of objectives possible if circumstances changed materially.

### Step 6: Final review and outcome

- One of three outcomes:
  - **Sustained improvement**: PIP closes successfully. Employee returns to standard performance management. Manager does a 30-day post-PIP check-in.
  - **Insufficient improvement, extension warranted**: PIP extended once, never more than once. Common in cases where external factors (illness, family crisis) intervened.
  - **Termination**: Per the Separation Policy.

### Step 7: Audit trail

- Documentation retained 7 years per HR Data Handling Supplement.
- Aggregate PIP outcomes reviewed quarterly by Maya Schwartz.

## Maya Schwartz's quarterly PIP audit

- Success rate (target: ≥40% sustained improvement; below 25% is a red flag suggesting PIPs are being used as a firing mechanism).
- Distribution by business unit, manager, demographic — to detect bias patterns.
- Average duration.
- Disparate-impact analysis with HR Legal.

## Anti-patterns

- PIP delivered cold without prior coaching.
- Objectives unmeasurable or unachievable.
- Manager unwilling to actually support the employee.
- "Stealth firing" — using PIP solely to document termination.
- Skipping the post-PIP 30-day check-in when PIP succeeded.
