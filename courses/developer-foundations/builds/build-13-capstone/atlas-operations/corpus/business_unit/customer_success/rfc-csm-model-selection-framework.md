# RFC · Customer Success Model Selection Framework

**Status:** Draft for discussion
**Owner:** Aisha Okonkwo (Director of Customer Success)
**Date:** 2028-09-01
**Audience:** Atlas customer-success leadership, Atlas commercial leadership, Atlas exec staff

## Problem statement

The Atlas customer-success function currently operates four distinct relationship models:

- Engineering-led (Priya Anand / Norvale)
- Compliance-led (Dr Sara Vance / Meridian)
- Operations-led (Marcus Ortiz / Halcyon; Aisha Okonkwo / Cresta)
- Field-ops-led (Liam Hayashi / Talos)

These models have evolved organically. There is no documented framework for choosing which model fits which customer. New customers are typically assigned to the default operations-led model, with subsequent re-modelling only if the relationship drifts.

This RFC proposes a structured framework for choosing the customer-success model at customer onboarding, and for re-evaluating at significant relationship inflection-points (e.g., new executive sponsor, major expansion).

## Proposed framework

A two-axis decision tree:

1. **Dominant relationship vector** — engineering, compliance, operations, or field-ops?
2. **Atlas-side bandwidth fit** — does the proposed Atlas-side executive partner have bandwidth for this customer?

If the two-axis evaluation produces a clear answer, assign that model. If not, default to operations-led.

## Detailed model selection criteria

### Engineering-led (Priya Anand pattern)

- Customer has strong in-house engineering culture (utilities, heavy industry).
- Customer-side decision-makers are engineering executives.
- Product complexity is engineering-dominant.
- Atlas-side VP Engineering has bandwidth.

### Compliance-led (Dr Sara Vance pattern)

- Customer is in a heavily-regulated industry (financial services, healthcare with audit dominance).
- Customer-side decision-makers are compliance or audit executives.
- Atlas-side CCO has bandwidth.

### Operations-led (default)

- Customer is in a standard enterprise context.
- Day-to-day operations are the dominant relationship surface.
- Standard CSM with operations background fits.

### Field-ops-led (Liam Hayashi pattern)

- Customer has substantial physical-fleet operational footprint (FieldOps-heavy, distributed sites).
- Customer-side decision-makers are operations executives with field background.
- Atlas-side Head of Field Operations has bandwidth.

## Re-evaluation triggers

- New customer-side executive sponsor.
- Expansion of >50% customer-side ARR.
- Sustained NPS drift of ≥10 points.
- Major product mix change (e.g., adding a compliance-heavy product to an operations-heavy customer).

## Open questions

1. How do we handle hybrid customers (e.g., Cresta, which is partly compliance-led and partly operations-led)?
2. What's the formal process for model transitions?
3. Should commercial team have veto on model assignment?
