# Customer Success Runbook · Post-Incident Trust Rebuild

**Owner:** Aisha Okonkwo (Director of Customer Success)
**Region:** Global
**Trigger:** Any P0 or P1 customer-facing incident that produces an NPS drop of ≥10 points or written customer-side relationship concern

## Origin

This runbook was codified from the Cresta Health Network trust-rebuild programme following INC-2028-0019 (see `case-study-cresta-recovery.md`).

## Phase 1 — Acknowledge (Days 0-2)

1. Within 48 hours of the incident closure, the CSM writes a personal acknowledgement to the customer's executive sponsor. No spin, no defensive framing.
2. Acknowledge the trust impact in concrete terms. If NPS dropped, name it.
3. Commit to no new-business pitch until the customer reopens the conversation themselves.

## Phase 2 — Listen (Weeks 1-4)

1. Schedule a monthly working session with the customer-side operational team (not just the executive sponsor).
2. Each session: 60-90 minutes, conversation-led, no slides.
3. Take notes, not actions. The goal is hearing.
4. CSM personally chairs every session for the first 90 days.

## Phase 3 — Demonstrate (Months 2-3)

1. Surface any product-side changes that resulted from the incident (e.g., POL-INCIDENT-v2.0 v2.0, MQTT broker HA architecture, firmware-gating sub-process).
2. Offer compliance partnership where relevant. For Cresta, this was the joint HIPAA audit-prep workstream.
3. Offer customer-controlled cadence on any future rollouts. Customer-side veto on firmware updates, deployment windows, etc.

## Phase 4 — Re-engage (Months 3-6)

1. Wait for the customer to reopen the new-business conversation.
2. When they do, treat it as a major relationship milestone.
3. Structure the new-business conversation around customer-controlled cadence.

## Indicators of success

- NPS recovery of ≥5 points within 90 days.
- Customer-initiated new-business conversation within 180 days.
- Customer-side executive sponsor quotes the CSM by name in written communications.

## Anti-patterns

- Discounting as a trust-rebuild lever. It rarely works and signals desperation.
- Bringing in new account team members during the rebuild phase. Continuity matters.
- Treating the rebuild as a "sales pause" rather than an active customer-success investment.

## Quote attributed to Aisha Okonkwo

> "Our job is not to prevent incidents. Our job is to make sure that when incidents happen, customers feel safer — not less safe — at the end."
