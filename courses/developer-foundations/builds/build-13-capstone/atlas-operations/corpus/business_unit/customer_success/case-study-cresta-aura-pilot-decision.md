# Case Study · Cresta Aura Pilot Approval as Trust Indicator

**Customer:** Cresta Health Network
**Region:** EMEA
**Primary CSM:** Aisha Okonkwo
**Decision date:** 2028-05-22

## Why the Aura pilot approval matters

Eleanor Brightwell's approval of the Atlas Aura pilot on 2028-05-22 was, in many ways, the single most important customer-success milestone of Atlas's first half of 2028. It marked the moment the Cresta relationship — damaged by INC-2028-0019 in February — turned around.

The pilot wasn't large in revenue terms. Four sites, a small piece of installed footprint. What mattered was the signal: Cresta was choosing to expand with Atlas, on Atlas's own product roadmap, three months after losing 19 days of facility-management stability.

## The path from no to yes

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 2028-02-09 | INC-2028-0019 begins |
| 2028-02-14 | First site rollback complete |
| 2028-02-21 | All sites rolled back. Incident closed. NPS = 52 |
| 2028-02-25 | Eleanor formally pauses the Aura conversation |
| 2028-03-04 | Atlas delivers HIPAA evidence pack |
| 2028-03-15 | Joint working session with Cresta CISO |
| 2028-04-10 | Eleanor mentions Aura might be on the table again |
| 2028-05-22 | Aura pilot approved (4 sites) |
| 2028-06-12 | Cresta external HIPAA audit passes clean |

## Why Eleanor came back

Three factors, in Eleanor's own words in the joint executive standup of 2028-05-22:

1. "Aisha didn't sell. She listened."
2. "The HIPAA evidence pack was Atlas treating our audit like it was Atlas's audit."
3. "The Cresta-specific firmware-gating sub-process means I'm in control of the rollout cadence, not Atlas."

## What this proves

- Customer trust dips are inflection-points, not endings.
- Compliance partnership (the HIPAA evidence pack) is a relationship-deepener.
- Customer-controlled rollout cadence is a powerful trust signal.
- The Cresta-specific firmware-gating sub-process is a customer-success commitment, not a product feature.

## Atlas-side takeaway

The Aura pilot decision was Eleanor Brightwell's decision. Aisha Okonkwo's job was to make sure Eleanor had everything she needed to make the decision freely. That distinction — between selling and enabling — is the heart of the Atlas customer-success function.
