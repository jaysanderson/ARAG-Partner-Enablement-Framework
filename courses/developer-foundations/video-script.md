# Video Script — Developer Foundations course trailer

> **Duration target:** 2 minutes
> **Format:** Screen recording + voiceover. Fast cuts, real artefacts only: dashboard, terminal, the course site. No stock footage, no slides.
> **Audience:** Partner developers deciding whether to start; partner managers deciding whether to enrol their team.

## Cold open (0:00 – 0:12)

**ON SCREEN:** Title card: *"Developer Foundations · Progress Agentic RAG."* Quick cut to a finished capstone running: branded chat answering, citations appearing.

**VOICEOVER:**
> Thirteen builds, one capstone. And at the end you can stand in front of a customer's CTO and ship a working RAG application on their actual content. That's Developer Foundations.

## What it is (0:12 – 0:40)

**ON SCREEN:** Split view: an AI coding assistant on the left being briefed, working code landing on the right. Cut to the brief being pasted, then the generated `ask.mjs` running with a cited answer in the terminal.

**VOICEOVER:**
> It's the on-ramp course for partners building on Progress Agentic RAG. And here's the thing: you won't hand-write much code. The whole course runs on vibe coding. You brief an AI assistant, Claude, Cursor, whatever you use, and your job is knowing what to ask for and checking what comes back. The platform does the retrieval and the citations. You direct.

## The journey (0:40 – 1:12)

**ON SCREEN:** Fast montage, one beat per build theme: dashboard "Upload folder" with the corpus dropping in → search and chat widgets on a real site → chat switching language mid-conversation → terminal printing clean JSON → the knowledge graph view → a residency settings page.

**VOICEOVER:**
> Build 0, you provision a Knowledge Box, drag a corpus in, and you're getting grounded answers inside the hour. From there it stacks. Drop-in search and chat on a real site. Multilingual chat that changes voice depending on who's asking. Structured outputs, real JSON your workflows can consume, not prose. Agents that enrich content at ingest. A typed knowledge graph. And the production conversation every CTO wants: residency, bring your own LLM, rate limits. You'll have those answers without checking your notes.

## Why it matters (1:12 – 1:38)

**ON SCREEN:** A clock graphic: *"0:30 — pick the primitive. 90:00 — working demo."* Cut to a demo running against a customer-looking corpus, cursor clicking a citation, the source document opening.

**VOICEOVER:**
> What the course is really teaching is fluency. A customer describes their content problem, you know which primitive solves it in thirty seconds, and ninety minutes later there's a working demo on their corpus, with citations they can click and check. That's what makes you valuable in the room.

## Capstone, cert, CTA (1:38 – 2:00)

**ON SCREEN:** Capstone montage: Atlas Operations and Aurora Concierge side by side. Then the course site landing page, cursor moving to **Start here → Build 0**. End card: *"Developer Foundations. Open the course. Start at Build 0."*

**VOICEOVER:**
> Pass the final exam, build the capstone, a 25-minute end-to-end demo you defend live, and you're a certified Practitioner. Your organisation needs one on staff to reach Authorized tier. Budget 25 to 40 hours across four to eight weeks. Everything you need is in the course. Open it, hit Start Here, and go build something.

---

## Production notes

- **Total VO is ~300 words.** Read at a natural pace it lands at 1:55 to 2:05. If it runs hot, trim the journey section first; the montage carries it visually.
- **Every visual is a real artefact.** Dashboard, terminal, the single-file course site, a running capstone. Nothing mocked. If a beat can't be shown for real, cut the beat, don't fake it.
- **The Build 0 ingest beat** (drag the corpus folder in) is the single most persuasive shot for developers. Give it a full two seconds of screen time.
- **End card** should show wherever the course file is hosted for partners. Set that URL before publishing.
